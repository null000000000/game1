# 01 — Brand Identity

**Project label:** Random Challenge Game (working title; not the final product name)  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Direction approved; name and logo remain open.

## 1. Brand essence

A tiny dose of surprise that turns an ordinary moment into a fun one.

**Core line:** Open. Reveal. Have fun.

The product is a lightweight, solo-first web game. It gives the user one small, doable challenge from a curated library of 100. It is not a social network, task manager, habit tracker, or user-generated challenge platform.

## 2. Positioning

For people who want a quick break, a laugh, or a small change of pace, the product reveals a simple challenge at random. Unlike productivity tools or open-ended social platforms, it offers a finite, curated set of safe, low-friction activities and makes the reveal itself entertaining.

## 3. Audience

- Primary: young adults and friends looking for a light activity during a break.
- Secondary: anyone who wants a quick, low-pressure prompt when bored.
- Usage context: short sessions on mobile first, with a responsive desktop experience.

## 4. Brand personality

| Trait | Direction | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Playful | Bright, surprising, expressive | Never noisy or chaotic |
| Friendly | Warm, conversational Arabic | Never childish or patronizing |
| Spontaneous | Unexpected reveal | Randomness must feel fair |
| Light | No pressure, no guilt | No streak anxiety or ranking |
| Clear | One obvious next action | No cluttered dashboards |

## 5. Voice and microcopy

Use short, friendly Egyptian Arabic as the initial content language. Keep button labels concise and action-led.

**Preferred examples**
- “يلا نلعب؟”
- “ورّيني التحدي”
- “تحديك النهارده”
- “خلصته! عاش 👏”
- “مش مناسب؟ جرّب واحد تاني”

**Avoid**
- Productivity language such as “حقق أهدافك” or “لا تضيع وقتك”.
- Shame, pressure, punishment, or competitive language.
- Claims that the user must complete a challenge.

## 6. Approved visual direction

**Direction:** Playful Minimal  
**Theme:** Light mode only for v1  
**Visual balance:** 70% calm neutral surfaces, 20% primary purple, 10% accent colors.

### 6.1 Color palette

| Token | Hex | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| `primary` | `#7667D9` | Main CTA, active states, brand anchor |
| `coral` | `#F28B82` | Social category, celebration accents |
| `mint` | `#91C9B7` | Explore category, positive secondary states |
| `yellow` | `#F4D58D` | Fun category, highlights |
| `sky` | `#73C9F5` | Brain & observation category |
| `background` | `#F8F7F3` | Main page background |
| `surface` | `#FFFFFF` | Cards and dialog surfaces |
| `text` | `#292638` | Main readable text |
| `muted` | `#777386` | Secondary text |
| `border` | `#E9E6F0` | Dividers and quiet outlines |
| `success` | `#5EAA83` | Completed state only |

Do not use the five bright colors at equal intensity across every screen. Purple owns the primary action; category colors are small semantic accents. The background remains warm off-white, not pure white everywhere.

### 6.2 Color accessibility

- Use dark text on pastel category surfaces; do not use white text on pale yellow, mint, coral, or sky.
- Primary purple may be used with white text for large buttons after contrast verification.
- Do not communicate category or state by color alone: include a text label or icon.
- Keep focus indicators visible and keyboard accessible.

### 6.3 Typography

- Arabic: **Cairo** or **IBM Plex Sans Arabic**. Choose one and use it consistently.
- Latin / technical labels: **Inter**.
- Headings: bold, compact, friendly; body copy: regular with comfortable line height.
- Avoid overly rounded display fonts that make the interface feel aimed at children.

### 6.4 Shape, depth, and illustration

- Cards: 20–24 px radius.
- Primary buttons: pill or 16–20 px radius; large tap target.
- Shadows: soft and restrained; use lift during interaction, not permanent heavy shadows.
- Use simple geometric shapes, sparkles, stars, confetti, and card motifs.
- Avoid stock-photo-heavy layouts. The game should be driven by typography, cards, and motion.

## 7. Logo direction

Logo is **not yet approved**. Do not invent or finalize a mark during implementation. Use a temporary typographic wordmark placeholder that can be replaced without redesigning the header. The final name and logo require a separate decision.

## 8. Experience principles

1. One main action per screen.
2. The reveal is the hero moment.
3. Every challenge is optional and low-pressure.
4. No signup wall before play.
5. Never make the user feel punished for skipping.
6. Motion should delight, not delay.
